# v1.2 addendum (from Phil's second hardware session, 2026-09-12)

Extends v1 and the v1.1 addendum. Phil's words: "so there's a fully featured app."

## A. Five more palettes (approved)
Add to `Palette.all`, each 4 to 6 colors plus a dim base, names sentence case:
- Blacklight: deep UV violets and electric purples (0x3A00FF, 0x6A00FF, 0x8F00FF, 0xB400FF, 0x2200AA), dim base a faint violet.
- Forest: mossy greens, teal, amber.
- Candy: pink, magenta, coral, mint, lemon.
- Ice: white-blue, cyan, pale violet.
- Fire: red, orange, amber, gold.
The palette picker scrolls or wraps so ten chips fit; selection persists.

## B. Bulb order and identify (approved)
- The bulb list supports drag and drop reordering (SwiftUI `onMove`), persisted via BulbNameStore order keyed by bulb id. Order is 1 to N top to bottom; show the number in the row.
- Each row has an "Identify" button (bolt icon): flashes that bulb white / off / white / off over about 1.5 s, then restores its previous state (using the last reported state; if none, leaves it white at the current brightness). Never runs while Party Mode is on (button disabled with a hint).
- Wave, Spread grouping, and the v1.2 Color flow scene all use the persisted order.

## C. Persistence audit (approved)
On relaunch the app restores: bulb names and order; every setting (menu bar, launch at login, rescan interval, updates per second, sensitivity, gate); the last effect and palette; the last scene and its speed; first-run flag. Party Mode and scenes start OFF on launch (spec 4.4: quitting never changes bulbs; launching never changes them either). Add a test that a fresh AppModel on the same store reproduces every value.

## D. Scenes (non-music modes) (proposed list, pending Phil's confirmation)
A new "Scenes" section in the main window below the bulb list, mutually exclusive with Party Mode (turning one on turns the other off). Each scene has a Speed slider (0.25x to 4x) and uses the palette picker; scenes run through the same BulbController stream path at 4 updates per second (they are slow by nature).
- Breathe: all bulbs brighten and dim together between the dim base and the palette color, one full breath per 6 s at 1x; the palette color advances each breath.
- Color flow: the palette flows bulb to bulb in list order, one bulb step per 2 s at 1x, smooth cross-fade.
- Candle: warm flicker per bulb (independent random walk in brightness 40 to 100 percent around 2200 K amber), updates 4 per second.
- Sunset: a 20 minute at 1x timeline from bright warm white (4000 K, 100 percent) to deep orange (2700 K equivalent RGB, 30 percent) to off; a progress bar shows time left; ends with bulbs off.
- Static: the palette spread across bulbs in order (color i mod count), no motion; a single color mode picks color 0 for all.
Scenes persist as "last scene" but do not auto-start on launch.

## Out of scope
Custom palettes, schedules, per-bulb scenes, BLE.

## E. Party brightness range (approved 2026-09-14)
Phil: "set a baseline darkness and brightness so it changes based on the level from that darkest to the brightest."
- Two settings, `partyFloor` (default 0.12) and `partyCeiling` (default 1.0), each 0 to 1, floor always <= ceiling minus 0.05 (clamp on save and in the UI). Persisted in GlowbeatSettings; sliders "Darkest" and "Brightest" in the Party panel under the Level row, and in Settings.
- Semantics: every effect produces a per-bulb color plus an implied intensity 0 to 1 (0 = calm, 1 = hit). The engine maps intensity through the range: output = paletteColor.scaled(by: floor + (ceiling - floor) * intensity). The palette dim base is no longer used by Party Mode (scenes may still use it). Below the gate, intensity is 0 (so bulbs sit at Darkest, not black, unless floor is 0).
- Implementation: extend the Effect protocol with `tick(...) -> [EffectOutput]` where `EffectOutput { color: RGB, intensity: Double }` (or keep `[RGB]` and add a parallel `intensities` accessor), migrate Pulse/Spread/Wave/Glow to report their existing level as intensity, and apply the range in PartyEngine before dedupe. Tests: floor 0.3 ceiling 0.6 with a beat gives brightness between 0.3 and 0.6 of the palette color; silence gives exactly floor; changing the range live takes effect on the next tick.

## F. Test hygiene (approved)

## I. Feel presets (approved 2026-09-14)

Phil: "simple presets that basically change the advanced settings. Like something for
fast and bright, low and slow, etc."

A `PartyPreset` enum (`App/Model/PartyPreset.swift`) of four feels. Each is exactly the
four values behind the Advanced disclosure and nothing else: Trigger Level, Always react,
Travel, the effect and the palette are never touched. Snap and Fade are stated as the
times they ask for and converted through `EffectTiming`, so the readouts print the
numbers in this table.

| Feel | Darkest | Brightest | Snap | Fade | Summary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Punchy | 0.10 | 1.00 | instant | 0.3 s | Fast and bright. |
| Mellow | 0.35 | 0.80 | 0.06 s | 1.5 s | Low and slow. |
| Dreamy | 0.20 | 0.70 | 0.25 s | 3.0 s | Soft hits, long settle. |
| Tight | 0.00 | 1.00 | instant | 0.1 s | Sharp flicker, goes dark between hits. |

(The Fade floor is 0.15 s and the readout prints one decimal, so Tight prints "0.1 s".)

- `GlowbeatSettings.matchingPreset` returns the feel the four current values sit on,
  within 0.005, or nil for **Custom**. Derived rather than stored, so moving any slider
  drops the row to Custom by itself and nothing can drift.
- `AppModel.applyPartyPreset(_:)` writes all four through the existing setters, so the
  running engine takes them live, and persists once.
- UI: a **Feel** row directly above the Advanced disclosure in the Party panel and above
  the four sliders in Settings. Four selectable segments plus a non-selectable Custom,
  with the chosen feel's summary as the caption underneath.
- **Answered 2026-09-14: Glowbeat ships on Punchy.** `GlowbeatSettings.defaults` takes
  its Darkest, Brightest, Snap and Fade from `PartyPreset.punchy` rather than retyping
  them, so a fresh install reads Punchy on the Feel row. `EffectTiming.standard` stays
  the Effects package's own default, for an effect built without the engine; the two are
  no longer the same numbers. Stored values are untouched, so nobody who has already
  moved a slider is moved off it.

## P. No tick marks under any slider (approved 2026-09-17)

Phil: "there are little tick marks under some of the sliders. remove all visual tick
marks."

- **The cause was never a design choice.** On macOS a `Slider(value:in:step:)` is drawn by
  an `NSSlider` with `numberOfTickMarks` set to one mark per step and
  `allowsTickMarkValuesOnly` on. Measured on macOS 26: a 1 through 15 range with a step of
  1 gives 15 marks, the same range with no step gives 0. There is no way to ask AppKit for
  the stepping without the marks.
- **Six sliders had a step**: Travel (Party pane), Shift over, Light up over, To and Dim
  over (Schedule pane), and Party Mode updates per second (Settings).
- **Every slider in the app is continuous now**, and the rounding happens in the binding.
  `LabeledValueSlider.step` is renamed `snapsTo`, deliberately not named after the
  parameter that draws the marks, and `LabeledValueSlider.snapped` is the one place the
  rounding lives: it clamps to the range, ignores a step of zero rather than dividing by
  it, refuses a value that is not finite, and measures its marks from the bottom of the
  range so a 1 through 100 slider snaps to 1, 2, 3 and not to 0, 1, 2.
- **Behavior is unchanged.** Travel still lands on whole bulbs and the ramps on whole
  minutes.
- **Two tests, from two directions.** One walks the real AppKit view tree behind every
  pane, Settings and the menu bar popover and asserts no `NSSlider` has a tick mark, with
  a companion test proving a stepped slider still asks for 15 of them so the rule cannot
  go vacuous. The other scans the app's own source, comments stripped, for a `Slider(`
  call carrying a `step:`, which catches a slider added to something the suite never
  renders. `ImageRenderer` cannot see any of this: it paints a placeholder glyph over
  every slider.

## Q. The Colors pane (approved 2026-09-17)

Phil: "another thing on the left sidebar ... a whole bunch of color presets for daylight,
evening, etc ... proper daylight balance, dusk, golden hour, sunrise, black light ... Be
creative ... individual static colors and a brightness slider so you can easily pick a
color, choose the brightness, and move on with life."

- **A fifth sidebar pane, between Scenes and Schedule.** The order is the order the panes
  read in: the bulbs themselves, then the three ways to light them loudest first, then
  when it happens without anyone asking. Symbol `paintpalette`. It keeps the bulb strip.
  Its status line is `SidebarStatus.colors`: "Golden hour, 70%" while a color is on the
  room, "Off" otherwise.
- **`StillColor`**, 26 colors in four groups, `Identifiable, Hashable, Sendable`, with
  either a Kelvin or an RGB. Whites go out as `colorwc` Kelvin, everything else as
  `colorwc` RGB, both through the one shot repeated sends, never through the Party stream.

  | Group | Colors |
  |---|---|
  | Whites | Daylight 6000 K, Overcast 6500 K, Studio 5000 K, Neutral 4000 K, Warm 3000 K, Candle 2700 K |
  | Sky | Sunrise `FF9E7A`, Golden hour `FFA114`, Dusk `C85A96`, Twilight `6A6BB4`, Moonlight `C8DCFF`, Midnight `1E3C8C` |
  | Mood | Blacklight `6A00FF`, Lava `FF3C0A`, Ember `B4500F`, Lavender `B48CFF`, Rose `FF5A82`, Mint `6EF0BE`, Ocean `00A0BE`, Forest `1E9646`, Ice `D2F5FF` |
  | Playful | Neon pink `FF1493`, Electric blue `0064FF`, Lime `8CFF14`, Tangerine `FF7800`, Grape `8C28DC` |

  Daylight takes its 6000 from `WhiteTemperature.daylightKelvin` rather than a second copy
  of the number, so this pane and the Schedule pane's Light card cannot drift into meaning
  two different whites. Blacklight is `Palette.blacklight`'s own violet. The values are
  tuned for a bulb, not a screen: a pale color needs all three channels high or it lands as
  a wash of its strongest one, and a deep one needs a little of the other two or it lands
  as pure red, green or blue.
- **A still color is not a driver.** Party Mode and the scenes repaint many times a
  second; this sends three commands and lets go. That is what leaves the room to the
  phone, the Bulbs pane or the next Light transition without an argument, and it is why
  the pane has no switch and no Apply button.
- **`AppModel.applyStillColor`** stops Party Mode, any scene, any ramp and any flash
  first, then sends on, the color, the brightness, in that order on one serial task: on
  first because a color sent to a dark bulb is a color nobody sees, brightness last
  because the bulb applies it to whatever it is currently showing.
- **`AppModel.setStillBrightness`** is the same drag and commit split the party sliders
  use, with a rate limit they do not need: each frame of this drag is a real UDP command
  to every bulb, so at most one lands every 150 ms while dragging and the release always
  lands. It sends nothing while Party Mode, a scene or a ramp owns the bulbs.
- **Two halves.** `settings.stillColorID` is the swatch the pane rings and is persisted;
  `AppModel.appliedStillColor` is what the room is wearing and is not, because a one shot
  leaves no way to know after a relaunch. The live half drops when Party Mode or a scene
  starts, when a sleep runs, when a Light mode is picked by hand, and on launch.
- **The Light mode does not fight it.** `LightModeEngine.userPickedAColor` makes the
  schedule brief's rule explicit: a color picked by hand stands until the next real
  transition, so a transition worked out while the music was playing is forgotten rather
  than flushed at the room on top of the color just asked for.
- **`GlowbeatSettings.stillColorID: String?`** (nil until anyone picks, and an id this
  build's catalog does not have is dropped on load) and **`stillBrightness: Double`**,
  0.01 through 1, default 0.7. One percent rather than zero: zero is a bulb that is off,
  and a brightness slider that could switch the room off would be a switch in a slider's
  clothes.
- **The pane** is the caption "Pick a color and it stays until you change it.", then the
  Brightness slider, then four groups under sentence case headings. The brightness is
  above the grid: 26 swatches do not fit the window, so a slider underneath would be the
  control you reach for after every click sitting behind the whole catalog. Measured, the
  pane is 783 points against 616 of room, so it scrolls by a little under two rows, and a
  test holds it to that.
- **A swatch** is 96 by 56 at radius 11, its name under it, with the hover lift and the

## S. Phone takeover: a color has to hold still (fix, 2026-09-23)

Phil, 2026-09-23: "It says a color was changed from the Govee app but nothing. I didn't do
anything. That's happened multiple times lately since I added more light bulbs." He had
gone from six bulbs to ten. This amends the color bullet of spec section 4.4 ("reported
color matches none of the last 5 colors the app sent to that bulb (with a tolerance of 8
per channel)"), which two polls in a row then paused on.

**Cause.** Five colors is half a second of Party Mode at ten sends a second, less than one
status poll. An H6004 fades to each `colorwc` color over 0.3 to 1 s instead of jumping,
so a poll can land between two sent colors. On a busy network (a hundred datagrams a
second across ten bulbs) a bulb can be a datagram or two behind, and a reply can come
back late. Each of those reads as "a color Glowbeat never sent", and ten bulbs polled
every second is ten chances a second.

| Rule | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Colors a report is matched against | The last 5 sent | Every color sent in the 3 s before the reply arrived (`BulbController.takeoverHistoryWindow`), plus the one the bulb held when those 3 s began, and never fewer than the last 5 however old |
| What counts as a match | Within 8 per channel of one of them | That, or within 8 per channel of the straight line from one sent color to the next (the firmware fade) |
| What pauses | 2 consecutive unexplained polls | 3 consecutive unexplained polls that also agree with each other within 8 per channel (`ColorMismatchRun`) |
| Power, brightness | First mismatch | Unchanged |

- **`SentColorHistory`** (GoveeLAN) is the per bulb history: every send stamped with the
  moment it went out, kept for two windows plus one entry and capped at 256, so a reply
  judged a moment late gets the answer it would have had on arrival.
  `BulbController.sentColorHistory(for:asOf:)` reads it; `recentSentColors(for:)` is now
  the last five entries of the same store, so its behavior is unchanged.
- **Why "never fewer than 5":** quiet music sends nothing new, so a bulb that missed the
  last few datagrams before a quiet passage keeps showing one of them for as long as the
  quiet lasts. A pure 3 s window would forget them and call it a phone. With the floor,
  the new set of explaining colors always contains the old one, so no report the old
  rule accepted can pause now.
- **Why the colors must agree:** a phone sets a color and leaves it. A lagging or fading
  bulb reports a different wrong color on every poll. Three polls agreeing is also the
  answer to fades the next send interrupts, which do not run along any one segment.
- **Brightness checked, left alone** (brief item 1d): Party Mode never sends a brightness
  command, only `colorwc`, so the bulb reports the session's baseline brightness on every
  poll. `testPartyModeSendsNoBrightnessSoBrightnessCannotFalsePause` holds that.
  **Superseded 2026-09-23 by section T:** Party Mode now sets every bulb to 100 as it
  starts, and a sent brightness is only judged once the bulb has shown it.
- **Cost:** a phone color change is noticed on the third poll, two to three seconds after
  it, not the second. And a phone color that happens to land within 8 of a color
  Glowbeat sent, or of the fade between two of them, in the last three seconds or the
  last five sends, is not seen while that stays true. Neither was worth ten bulbs
  pausing for nothing, and every pause the new rule makes, the old one made too.
- **Not hardware verified.** Whether `devStatus` reports the color part way through a
  fade, or the color the bulb is heading for, has never been measured. Either way the
  window and the steady rule cover it: the fade check only ever adds matches.

## T. The room's send budget, real full brightness, and one ordered stop (fixes, 2026-09-23)

Three fixes from the smoothness investigation (`smoothness-investigation-report.md`, fixes
1 and 5) and Phil's "the bulbs rarely reach actual 100% very much". Brief:
`network-brightness-brief.md`. Amends spec 4.4 (polling, the takeover check, stop).

### T1. A send budget for the whole room (Phil approved)

Before: each bulb capped at the Settings rate (10 a second) and nothing capped the room.
Ten bulbs were a hundred `colorwc` a second in bursts of ten inside 10 ms, plus ten polls
and ten replies; six bulbs at ten a second (sixty) had felt smooth.

| | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Room budget | none | `StreamRateLimiter.roomBudgetPerSecond = 60` |
| Per bulb rate | the Settings value | `min(ceiling, max(2, 60 / bulbs))`: 6 bulbs 10/s, 10 bulbs 6/s, 15 bulbs 4/s |
| Engine tick rate | the Settings value | the per bulb rate, worked out again live when a bulb joins or leaves or the slider moves |
| Inside a tick | every bulb in one burst | each bulb at its own slot, `20 ms x place / bulbs`, never closer than half a slot, never across ticks, same serial chain, the tick's own timestamp |
| Party poll interval | 1 s | `max(1 s, bulbs / 6)`: 1 s up to six bulbs, 1.7 s at ten, 2.5 s at fifteen |
| Settings slider | "Party Mode updates per second", the rate | "Most updates per bulb, per second", a ceiling; readout "10 (6 with 10 bulbs)" when the room lowers it; caption "Glowbeat lowers this automatically when you have many bulbs, so the network keeps up." |

- **Why slots and not a gap between senders:** a bulb's slot is its place in the room, so
  its own sends stay exactly one tick apart whichever other bulbs the deduper skipped.
  Staggering bulbs across ticks was simulated and rejected (half the room shows the old
  hue half the time).
- **Why the polls too:** every poll is a request and a reply on the same Wi-Fi. Six a
  second for the room is what the six bulb room that felt smooth sent.
- **Cost, stated:** a phone is caught within one poll interval for power and brightness
  and within two to three for a color. Up to six bulbs that is unchanged (1 s, 2 to 3 s);
  at ten, 1.7 s and 3.3 to 5 s; at fifteen, 2.5 s and 5 to 7.5 s, plus a reply's trip.
  Fewer, bigger steps per bulb: at 6/s the per send brightness step is about 10 points,
  which Phil's own 6/s test found smooth thanks to the H6004's own fade.
- **More than thirty bulbs** stay at 2/s each and go over the budget: slower no longer
  reads as motion.
- Shipped ceiling stays 10. Phil's stored 6 is left alone.

### T2. Party Mode and scenes set the bulbs' own brightness to 100

Party Mode dims in the colors it sends (Darkest to Brightest), which assumes the bulb's
own brightness is 100. The Colors pane, the All bulbs slider, the menu bar slider and the
schedule set that brightness, so a room left at 30 percent ran the session at 30 percent
of Brightest.

- **Party start** (once the audio tap is open, so a session that never starts brightens
  nothing) and **Resume** send a one shot `setBrightness(100)` to every target through the
  engine's command chain, ahead of the first streamed color. A bulb that joins a running
  session gets it too. It repeats like every one shot (a second and two seconds later).
- **Every scene** streams RGB with its brightness in the color (Breathe, Color flow,
  Candle, Sunset, Static), so `SceneEngine.start` does the same through its own chain.
  None of them uses the bulb's hardware brightness on purpose.
- **Stop does not restore the brightness.** The only record of it is a status report from
  before the session, possibly a minute stale; putting back a guess would dim the settle
  color, or the light mode's white, to a level nobody chose.
- **Takeover (amends section S's brightness bullet):**
  `ExternalChangeDetector.BrightnessCheck`. A brightness Glowbeat sent is only judged once
  the bulb has reported it at least once this session; until then the report is not judged
  on brightness (power and color still are). Every report counts, the session's baseline
  included. Each new value Glowbeat sends has to be shown again, which also closes the
  "slider dragged mid session" race section S left open. Tests: a bulb reporting 30 then
  100 never pauses; a phone at 40 after the bulb reported 100 pauses on the first poll.
- **Cost:** a phone brightness change in the first seconds of a session is not caught:
  Glowbeat's own repeats put it back, and before the bulb has shown 100 it is not judged.

### T3. One ordered stop path

On stop with the light mode on Auto, the white it held back went down the light mode's
chain while the settle color went down Party Mode's, behind the last tick; the settle
landed last on every bulb and the room ended dim purple instead of white (investigation
H3). A still color picked mid session raced the same settle color, and the last tick.

- **The rule.** When Party Mode stops, the light mode wins if it has a white waiting (a
  transition, or a mode picked, while the music played): the room ends on that white.
  Otherwise the palette's dim base, as spec 4.4 has always had it. A still color picked
  while Party Mode runs is what the room ends on.
- **One path.** `AppModel.switchPartyModeOff(settles:)` is the only way Party Mode goes
  off. `PartyEngine.stop(settle:)` skips the dim base when the next owner is known (held
  colors are still flushed), and `pendingCommands` is the tail of its command chain.
  `LightModeEngine.setSuspended(_:after:)` queues its white on its own chain behind that
  tail; the still color's commands wait for it too. So whatever paints the room next is
  the last thing a bulb hears.
- **Not changed:** a scene started after Party Mode, and a sleep timer stopping Party
  Mode, still run on their own chains. A scene's first color is a quarter second away, far
  behind Party Mode's last command; a 0 minute sleep's power off can still land before the
  white or the settle color if Party Mode's last tick is mid spread, as the settle color
  already could before this change.
- **Not hardware verified.** None of T1 to T3 has run against the bulbs. Checklist Step 4d.

## U. The feel: colors glide, beats hit; Spread bass at full; Confetti (approved 2026-09-23)

Phil's decisions after the smoothness investigation (H2: the room read as color hopping at
constant brightness, not as pulsing). Brief: `feel-confetti-brief.md`.

- **The beat moves the brightness, the phrase moves the color.** Pulse, Spread and Wave
  share a `ColorClock` (Effects): the palette moves on every 4 low beats, never sooner
  than 0.8 s after the last change, and the new color crossfades in over 0.4 s (RGB,
  blended per tick). Every low beat is still a full hit on Snap and Fade exactly as
  before. Wave pushes the clock's color: the first bulb follows the fade, and each cell
  keeps the color it was pushed with, so a new color travels in as a band. Glow already
  drifted continuously and is unchanged.
- **The gate glides.** When the music drops below Trigger Level the room glides to calm
  over the user's Fade (`GateGlide`, App): a ceiling starting at the room's brightest bulb
  falls at `elapsed / release` per tick, and each bulb is shown at the lesser of what the
  effect asks and that ceiling. The effect keeps ticking underneath, so Wave's last
  highlight keeps traveling as it fades. Reopening lifts the ceiling at once.
- **Spread's bass at full.** `bassIntensityScale` (0.6) is gone; every group reports the
  whole hit. Bass is told apart by hue alone: `SpreadEffect.bassTone` leans the palette
  color 40 percent (up to 50, to differ from mid by at least 24 on some channel) toward
  the nearest darker palette color in hue within 90 degrees. The darkest color in its
  corner of the wheel leans toward its nearest hue instead (Party's red toward its
  orange); a one color palette toward its own saturated shade. Never scaled down. On the
  ten palettes bass is deeper than mid for 35 of 47 colors and always within 45 degrees
  of mid's hue.
- **Confetti** (`partyConfetti`, off by default): a switch under the Palette grid in the
  Party pane and under the palette picker in the menu bar popover, caption "Every bulb
  gets its own color from the palette, and never matches its neighbors." On the `Effect`
  protocol, live, kept through a change of effect and a resume. `ConfettiScatter` gives
  each bulb a palette position with no two neighbors (in bulb order) equal, re-scattered
  only when the color clock moves, crossfading bulb by bulb. At a change it also keeps
  every pair of neighbors at least 8 apart on some channel for the whole fade: Fire and
  Blacklight are nearly straight lines in RGB, so two fades can otherwise cross. Solved
  exactly along the row; as few bulbs as possible keep their color. Two color palettes
  alternate and flip at each change. Per effect: Pulse flashes every bulb in its own
  color; Spread gives each bulb its own color in its band's shade; Wave pushes every new
  cell in a color different from the cell behind it; Glow drifts each bulb from its own
  scattered start.
- **Window.** The Confetti row made the palette column the taller of the two by 66 points,
  so the window opens at 1000 by 850 (was 820), measured the same way as before. Still
  Phil's call against the 760 mockup and a scroller.
- **Simulator, Phil's settings** (gate 0.615, Snap 1, Fade 0.79 s, Darkest 52 percent,
  6/s, Blacklight, 10 bulbs), four tracks, before and after:

  | Measure | Before | After |
  |---|---|---|
  | Pulse color changes a second, dance / hip hop / ambient / jazz | 3.88 / 1.33 / 1.07 / 1.22 | 0.96 / 0.33 / 0.26 / 0.30 |
  | Largest one tick hue step, delta E (per channel) | 30.2 (122) | 13.8 (51) |
  | Gate close, brightest bulb's one tick drop p90, Pulse / Spread / Wave (points) | 48.4 / 48.4 / 48.4 | 10.3 / 10.3 / 10.3 |
  | Spread bass bulb, brightest | 80.7 percent | 100 percent |
  | Confetti, neighbor ticks sharing a color (Pulse and Spread, 4 palettes) | n/a | 0 |

- **Not hardware verified.** Nothing ran against the bulbs. The H6004's own `colorwc` fade
  runs on top of the 0.4 s software crossfade; whether the two together read as one
  smooth change or as a slow one is for Phil's eyes (checklist Step 4 and the Confetti
  sub-step).
