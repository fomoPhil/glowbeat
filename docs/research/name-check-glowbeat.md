# Name check: "Glowbeat"

**Date:** 2026-09-14
**Subject:** Is "Glowbeat" / "GlowBeat" / "Glow Beat" available for a macOS app that makes Govee smart bulbs react to music?
**Verdict:** **Use with caution, and rename now if you are going to.** Legally low risk. Commercially crowded in exactly our category, with no ownable domain.

---

## 1. Bottom line

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is there a registered trademark for GLOWBEAT? | **No. Zero records worldwide, live or dead, any class, any office.** |
| Is anyone using the name commercially? | **Yes, at least seven parties. Three of them sell light-plus-sound products.** |
| Is there a Mac App Store app called Glowbeat? | **No.** |
| Is there an iOS app called Glowbeat? | **Not any more.** One recently renamed away from it. |
| Is there a GitHub project called glowbeat? | **Yes, and it is a music-to-light engine.** Direct functional twin. |
| Can we own glowbeat.com? | **No, not cheaply.** Registered 2017, parked on BuyDomains as a premium listing. |
| Can we own glowbeat.app? | **No.** Taken and live with a real site. |
| What is actually available? | getglowbeat.com, glowbeatapp.com, github.com/glowbeat, npm/PyPI/crates, Homebrew. |

**The legal risk is low. The branding risk is the real problem.** We would launch into a search-results page owned by LED Bluetooth speakers, with no matching domain, next to an identically named open-source project that does the identical thing.

## 6. Recommendation

### Verdict: use with caution. My advice is to rename, and to do it now.

**The case for keeping Glowbeat:**
- The trademark register is genuinely, completely empty. That is rare and valuable. A US intent-to-use filing in classes 9 and 42 would face no cited prior mark.
- No Mac App Store or iOS App Store collision today.
- The name is a good name: two syllables that say exactly what the app does, easy to say, easy to spell, no ambiguity.
- The strongest commercial user (Glowbeat Audio) is defunct, and the rest are dropshippers on default subdomains with weak, probably unprovable rights.
- The project is already scaffolded as `Glowbeat.xcodeproj`, bundle `com.philwoolley.glowbeat`, with GoveeLAN / AudioTap / Effects packages. There is real (if small) switching cost.

**The case against, which I find stronger:**

1. **No ownable domain.** glowbeat.com is a paid premium listing and glowbeat.app is live with a real site. "getglowbeat.com" is a compromise we would be stuck explaining forever.
2. **Google is already owned by the wrong category.** Search "glowbeat" and you get LED Bluetooth speakers, a glow-party dropshipper, and a soundbar LED feature. Those are *adjacent* to us, which is worse than being unrelated: a customer searching our name lands on a $40 speaker with reactive lights and concludes they found it.
3. **An active open-source project has our name and our function.** `glowbeat-rs` is literally "a music-to-light engine with Swift bindings," pushed one week ago. Zero stars today, but it will confuse every technical person who searches, and it is Apache-2.0, so it will outlive its author's interest.
4. **Common-law exposure sits in class 9.** Bluetooth speakers are class 9 goods. "GlowBeat Portable LED Bluetooth Speaker" is a prior class 9 commercial use in the light-plus-music category. An examiner would not block our registration, but a prior user could oppose it. The probability is low; the fact that three separate parties sell light-plus-sound products under this name means it is not zero.
5. **The name is unregistered precisely because it is obvious.** GLOWBEAT is empty on the register not because nobody thought of it, but because at least seven parties reached for it independently and none of them thought it was distinctive enough to protect. That is a signal about the name, not an opportunity.

**Cost of renaming right now is close to nothing:** pre-launch, no App Store Connect record, no users, no press, no backlinks. It is a find-and-replace across `project.yml`, the bundle ID, the entitlements file, and the Xcode project, plus a git mv. After the first TestFlight build goes out and certainly after launch, that cost climbs sharply.

**If you keep it anyway,** do these three things: file a US ITU application in classes 9 and 42 immediately (the name is unclaimed, filing first converts us from "fourth squatter" to "the registrant"), buy getglowbeat.com and glowbeatapp.com defensively, and accept that we will never rank first for our own name.

### Per-name detail

**1. Roomraver** *(my pick)*
- App Store: 0 near-matches across 15 results (all "room planner" and "roommate" noise).
- GitHub: `total_count: 0` for both repos and users.
- Domains: roomraver.com RDAP 404, getroomraver.com 404, roomraver.app no NS records. All three free. npm 404.
- Web: no exact-match brand anywhere. Nearest are "Rave Room Recordings" (an Olly James label) and "The Raver Room" (a rave clothing store). Different word order, different category.
- No confusion with Lightjams, Light DJ, or iLightShow. No collision with Hue, Nanoleaf, LIFX, or Govee branding.
- **Why I like it:** it describes the emotional outcome, not the mechanism. The app does not make lights blink; it turns your room into a rave. That is the product promise in one word. Risk: "raver" skews EDM and party, which may feel narrow if the app also wants to do ambient and chill scenes.

**2. Gloamwave**
- App Store: no exact or near match (8 fuzzy results, all "brainwave" and "nightwave" noise).
- GitHub: 0 repos, 0 users. Genuinely zero.
- Domains: gloamwave.com 404, getgloamwave.com 404, gloamwave.app 404 with no NS. All free. npm 404.
- Web: no entity found at all. Nearest neighbors are different words: the band **Gloam** (Perth shoegaze) and **Gloamwalker** (a Bandcamp synth project).
- **Cleanest string of all thirteen tested.** But "gloam" means dusk or twilight, so it reads dim and muted, which is the opposite of vibrant color reacting to music. Also low spellability for a general consumer. Naming risk, not legal risk.

**3. Lumencade**
- App Store: no match. The 14 results are Lutron, LotusLamp, and third-party Hue apps, which is our actual competitive set, none named this.
- GitHub: 0 repos, 0 users.
- Domains: lumencade.com 404, getlumencade.com 404, lumencade.app 404 with no NS. All free. npm 404.
- Web: no exact hit, but the near-misses are all in lighting: **LumenCache Inc** (LED and low-voltage lighting), **Lumenade** (lighting design software, lumenade.com), **Lumenpulse** (Lumenfacade luminaires). Apple Arcade also shipped a game called **lumen.**
- Moderate risk. "Lumen" is the SI unit for light, so it is semi-descriptive in this category and sits in a dense field. Exact-string conflict: none. Distinctiveness: weaker than it first looks, and semi-descriptive marks are harder to register and harder to defend.

**4. Glimmerdrop**
- App Store: 0 matches. Nearest is "Glimmer", a social app by Glimmer World Pty Ltd.
- GitHub: 0 repos, 0 users.
- Domains: glimmerdrop.com 404, getglimmerdrop.com 404, glimmerdrop.app 404 with no NS. All free. npm 404.
- Trademark: no GLIMMERDROP found. Nearest registered: GLIMMERSOFT (E&E Co), GLIMMERJAM, GLIMMR.
- **The catch:** "Glimmerdrop" is a craftable bug in **Infinity Nikki**, with wall-to-wall fandom SEO (two Fandom wikis, Game8, GamerGuides, GameRant, TikTok). We would never rank page one for our own app name. Not a legal conflict, a discoverability one.

**5. Shimmerdrop**
- App Store: 1 unrelated 2015 game. No match.
- GitHub: 0 repos; one user account exists at github.com/Shimmerdrop.
- Domains: shimmerdrop.com 404, getshimmerdrop.com 404, shimmerdrop.app 404 with no NS. All free. npm 404.
- Web: **Shimmer Drops Paint** (shimmerdropspaint.com), an Australian handmade watercolor brand with roughly 131K Instagram followers at @shimmerdropspaint. "Shimmer drops" is also descriptive in cosmetics.
- Different Nice class (art supplies and cosmetics vs software), so low legal risk. The real cost is search and social: we would be the second Shimmerdrop on both Google and Instagram.
